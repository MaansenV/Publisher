Make the body believable in first person: the head looks where you look, the hands hold items, the feet stay on the ground and the fingers wrap around what you carry. This guide shows which component does each job and which settings you will change.

The Setup Wizard adds the model components (except footsteps). The lower the order, the earlier the component runs, so the head is solved before the hands.

Head and spine

PlayerHeadIK turns the head toward the look direction. Its weights decide how much of the look goes to the body, head, eyes, and how far the head may turn:

weight float default 1
Overall strength. SetWeight and SetWeightSmooth change it at runtime.
bodyWeight, headWeight, eyesWeight default 0, 1, 0
Share of the look that the body, head and eyes take. SetWeights sets all of them and the clamp together.
clampWeight float default 0.5
How far the head may turn away from the body.

PlayerSpineStabilizer keeps the spine upright while the head moves. It is configured in PlayerSettings:

enableSpineStabilization bool default true
Turns the stabilizer on or off.
spineStiffness float default 1
Strength of the correction. 0 switches it off.
spineDamping float default 10
Smoothing speed of the correction.
lowerSpineWeight, middleSpineWeight, upperSpineWeight default 0.3, 0.3, 0.4
How much each spine segment takes part in the correction.

Hands and holding items

The hands are driven by PlayerHandIK. Where an item sits in your hands comes from its ItemAttachmentData, described in Items: Grips and IK. Global limits come from PlayerSettings:

handVerticalLimitUpper, handVerticalLimitLower float default 45 / 45
Degrees of look up and down before the hand stops following the view. Items can override them.

The holster timings (holsterDuration, drawDuration, drawStartWeight) are described in Items: Equip, switch, holster and use. Two-handed items fade the left hand on and off over twoHandedBlendDuration (default 0.2 seconds).

Hand sway and walking motion

The held hand is not rigid. It trails the view when you turn, breathes at rest, bobs with each step and leans into strafes. Both parts are set on PlayerHandIK and are on by default:

sway HandSway Look Lag & Breathing
The hand follows the view on a damped spring with a slight overshoot. It breathes, faster and deeper under exertion when PlayerVitals is present, and drifts slowly while idle.
gait HandGait Walking
The hand bobs in step with the animated head, drags when you start or stop, and the item rolls into strafes.

Each part has an enabled switch. These are the main settings; the defaults suit most characters:

sway.frequency default 3.5 Hz
Spring frequency of the look lag. Higher is tighter and shorter.
sway.damping default 0.6
Damping ratio. 1 has no overshoot. 0.6 overshoots by about 10 %.
sway.lagScale default 0.6
Share of the lag shown on the hand. 0 keeps the hand rigid.
sway.yawPositionShare default 0.6
Share of the sideways lag on the hand position, so left and right move like up and down.
sway.restRate, sway.exertedRate default 0.25 and 0.65 breaths per second
Breathing rate at rest and at full exertion.
gait.bobFrequency default 4 Hz
Spring frequency of the step bob. Lower gives a bigger bob. Keep it well above the sprint step rate of about 3 Hz.
gait.bobGain default 2.5, 1, 1
Bob gain per axis: x right, y up, z forward.
gait.inertiaGain default 0.25
Share of the start and stop drag that the hand shows.
gait.strafeRoll default 2.5 degrees per m/s
Roll of the item into a strafe. A negative value flips the direction.

In the Unity Editor, both fade out while you calibrate an item with Debug Mode on in its Item Hold Data, and fade back in afterwards. Scripts can call PlayerHandIK.SetSwaySuppressed(bool). SwayWeight reports the current strength, where 0 means a rigid hand. View jumps such as a respawn or teleport go through PlayerCameraController.WarpLookDirection, so the hand snaps instead of swinging across the screen.

Foot planting

PlayerFootPlanting keeps each foot anchored to the ground where it was set down. While you stand or turn in place, a foot that moves too far from its anchor takes a short curved re-step to a new anchor, instead of sliding. Feet also align to the ground height and slope, and the pelvis lowers so both feet reach uneven ground. The component uses its own leg solver and runs before the camera syncs to the head.

Set it up

  1. Check that PlayerFootPlanting is on the model. The Setup Wizard adds it in step 4.
  2. Make sure Ground Layers includes the floor. The default is the Default layer only.
  3. Enter Play Mode and walk over an uneven surface or turn in place to test it.

Basic settings

The Beginner view shows the settings most projects need:

enablePlanting Enable Foot Planting, default on
Turns the whole system on or off. PlayerController.SetFootIKEnabled does the same at runtime.
plantMode PlantMode default Automatic
Idle and Moving fix the planting mode. Automatic (default) switches between them with the locomotion state, using idle hysteresis.
groundLayers LayerMask default Default layer
The layers the feet stand on.
stepSettings.stepDuration Step Duration, default 0.375
Seconds per re-step.
stepSettings.minLiftHeight, maxLiftHeight Min / Max Foot Lift, default 0.1 / 0.4
How high the foot lifts during a re-step.

Advanced settings

Click Advanced Settings to show the foldouts:

  • Planting: anchorCaptureRange (default 0.375) is how far the animated foot may move from its anchor before it is released. releaseAngle (30 degrees) releases a planted foot that twists too far. maxLegExtension (0.99) pulls the anchor back when the leg is stretched too far.
  • Ground Align: alignFeetToGround (on), maxFootYaw (30 degrees) and the speed of ground alignment.
  • Ground Probe: groundLayers, triggerInteraction (ignores triggers by default) and probeLength (default 1).
  • Pelvis: pelvisFollowWeight (1), pelvisDropWeight (0.5) and pelvisRaiseLimit (0) and minLegExtension (0.55): with the feet at different heights, the pelvis drops less so the higher leg does not fold into a deep squat.
  • Re-Steps: the StepSettings curves for path, arc, lift and rotation, plus arcStrength (0.3), catchUpBoost (0.4) and restepDistance (0.055).
Remote players

Multiplayer add-ons can feed the drive state of a remote player through SetExternalDriveState. The PurrNet add-on does this. Details are in PurrNet.

Turning and stopping

Planted feet keep their heading while the body turns. Past maxFootYaw (30 degrees by default, in the Ground Align foldout) a planted foot pivots on its ball and its heel lifts, instead of twisting at the ankle. Re-steps lead the turn, and a foot that is left turned after you stop settles with a re-step. These settings are in the Turning & Stopping foldout of Advanced Settings:

plantedHeadingLock default 1
How strongly planted feet keep their heading. 0 lets them swivel with the body. It needs maxFootYaw above 0.
kneeFollowsFoot default 0.6
How much the knee turns with a foot that points away from the body, so the leg turns at the hip instead of at the ankle.
turnLead, maxTurnLead default 0.12 s and 25 degrees
How far ahead of the turn a re-step aims, and the limit of that lead.
settleYaw, settleDistance, settleDelay default 10 degrees, 0.07, 0.4 s
A foot left turned or displaced after the body stops takes a settle re-step after the delay. 0 turns the yaw or the distance check off.
plantOnStop default on
When you stop, a foot plants where it touches the ground instead of sliding into the idle pose.
footGap default 0.06
Least gap between the feet during and after a re-step, in leg lengths. In a fast turn the stepping foot lands beside the standing foot instead of on its spot.

Stride warping

Stride warping scales the step length to the body's real speed, so a standing foot moves back as fast as the body moves instead of sliding. It needs the stride table, which holds one speed per clip of the animator's base layer. The table is empty until you bake it:

  1. Select the player, as a scene object or a prefab, that has PlayerFootPlanting.
  2. Run Tools › FBSystem › Foot Planting › Bake Stride Speeds, or press Bake Stride Speeds in the Stride foldout of Advanced Settings.
  3. Bake again after you change the locomotion clips.

The bake samples each clip on a hidden copy of the character, so the character itself is not posed. The Setup Wizard bakes the table while it connects the systems, if the table is still empty. With an empty table, stride warping is off.

strideWarp default 1
Strength of stride warping. 0 turns it off.
minStrideScale, maxStrideScale default 0.5 and 1.4
Shortest and longest step against the clip's step. A body slower than the minimum slides its feet instead of shuffling. Past the maximum the legs would not reach, so a faster body slides its feet.

Finger grips

HandPoseController sets the fingers around the held item on top of the animation. It reads the finger limits from the Humanoid avatar, so it works on any humanoid rig with mapped finger bones. Press Calibrate in its Inspector after you change the avatar. Items without a grip keep the animated fingers.

A grip has two parts. A FingerPose asset is a reusable template. Per item, you add tweaks on top of the template.

  1. Create a template with Create › Player › Finger Grip Pose. Set the five finger curls (0 open, 1 closed), thumbOpposition (wrap of the thumb) and spread.
  2. Select the item's ItemHoldData and open Finger Grip. Assign the right-hand template. For two-handed items, assign the left-hand template as well.
  3. Adjust the item with the tweaks: Hand Tweak (whole hand), Index Tweak and Thumb Tweak. The tweaks stay on the item and never change the shared template. Reset Tweaks clears them.
  4. Click Open Preview to see the grip on the player in Edit Mode. Use Save As New Template… to keep a tweaked grip as a new template.

Grips follow the item that is visible. During a holster animation, the fingers change with the item.

Footsteps

PlayerFootsteps plays a sound when a foot lands. It reads the animated feet, so no animation events are needed. Add it to the player root by hand, because the Setup Wizard does not add it.

  1. Add PlayerFootsteps to the player root.
  2. Assign Step Clips (stepClips). One clip is picked at random for each step.
  3. Optional: assign Re-Step Clips (reStepClips) for steps taken while turning in place. Leave it empty to use the step clips.
stepVolume default 0.6 · reStepVolume default 0.3
Volume of walking steps and of re-steps.
pitchRange default 0.92 to 1.08
Random pitch per step.
minRootSpeed default 0.3 m/s
Slower movement stays silent. Steps are silent while airborne or standing still.
minIntervalPerFoot, minIntervalGlobal default 0.2 s and 0.08 s
Minimum time between steps of one foot, and between any two steps.

Your own effects can listen to FootPlanted (C#, receives FootStepArgs) or to OnFootPlanted (UnityEvent). Both fire for walking steps and re-steps.

Common pitfalls

Stride warping does nothing

The stride table is empty. Bake it as described in Stride warping.

The hand lags or bobs too much

Lower sway.lagScale or gait.bobGain on PlayerHandIK, or turn the part off with its enabled switch. See Hand sway and walking motion.

Feet slide or float

Check Ground Layers on PlayerFootPlanting. Make sure no other foot IK runs on the same character.

Foot settings were reset after an update

Version 1.6 renamed the foot component and its settings. Re-enter your values. See 1.6.0.

Hands in the wrong place

Tune the item's grip and hand offsets. Use Tune an item in the scene and save the result as an IK preset.
